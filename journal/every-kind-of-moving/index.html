Every kind of moving counts

StepHaven is a game you play by moving, and walking isn't the only way to move. Here's how wheelchair pushes and workouts earn points too.

October 16, 2026 · Updated November 11, 2026 · Brody Richmond

In StepHaven, the steps your iPhone counts become points you spend on a cozy little town. That works well if you walk. It leaves out anyone who gets around in a wheelchair, and anyone whose exercise doesn't come in steps, like a bike ride, a swim or a rowing session. So StepHaven has two more ways to earn.

Is there a walking game that counts wheelchair pushes?

Yes. StepHaven, the cozy walking game for iPhone, counts wheelchair pushes recorded by an Apple Watch. Each push earns two points, where a step earns one, so a typical day of pushing builds about as much as a typical day of walking.

You play StepHaven on your iPhone, and your Apple Watch supplies the push count.

An Apple Watch in wheelchair mode counts pushes instead of steps, and it saves them to Apple Health. Turn on Wheelchair Pushes in StepHaven's settings, under Ways to Earn, (or tick "I use a wheelchair" while setting up) and those pushes earn points.

Each push is worth two points. Those points buy the same buildings, paths and decorations as points earned by walking. There is no separate catalog for wheelchair users, and no daily movement target you must meet to keep what you have built.

StepHaven isn't the only game that counts pushes. Pikmin Bloom has a wheelchair mode too, and its help page says it converts one push to 0.5 steps. StepHaven pays a push twice what it pays a step, because people make fewer pushes in a day than walkers make steps. At two points a push, a day of pushing lands about where a day of walking does.

Once pushes are on, the game stops calling your points "steps". The counter shows what you have to spend, prices show plain numbers, and the widgets say pushes on days when you've pushed more than you've walked.

StepHaven only asks Health for push data after you switch this on, so people who don't use it never see an extra permission request.

Ember: workouts that don't come in steps

Ember is how other exercise turns into points. Before you start a workout, you light an Ember for 15, 30, 45, 60 or 90 minutes. While it burns, StepHaven reads eligible exercise minutes and active calories recorded by Apple devices in Apple Health. Ember works best with Apple Watch: a workout needs the qualifying Health data, not just a manually entered workout name or duration.

You can light one from the app, from the Ember widget on your Home Screen, from Control Center, with the Action button, or by asking Siri. While it's burning, a Live Activity on your Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island shows how long is left and how much you've earned.

Why you light it first

We could have counted every calorie all day automatically. We chose not to, for two reasons.

  • It's a moment. Lighting an Ember before a ride or a swim feels like deciding to do it, and the town is waiting when you get back.
  • It's fair to walkers. A minute that already earned points as steps doesn't pay twice. If you light an Ember and go for a walk, you get the steps, and Ember only adds what the steps missed.

The limits, plainly

RuleAmount
Length of one Ember15 to 90 minutes
Ember time a dayup to 120 minutes
Points paid in fullup to 5,000 a day
After thathalf rate, to 8,000

These are game earning limits, not exercise goals. Only qualifying exercise minutes and active calories recorded by Apple devices count; manually entered activity and calories from other apps do not qualify. Points from an Ember appear in your balance as it burns. They're settled about an hour after it goes out, which gives your Watch time to finish syncing.

Do indoor movement and quiet days count?

StepHaven reads recorded movement, not a route on a map. Indoor steps or pushes can count when your device records them in Apple Health. A device left behind cannot record your movement, and recording accuracy depends on the activity and how you carry or wear it. Nothing you have built disappears when you take a quiet day.

For walking and offline-play details, see the cozy walking guide. To contribute your own points to a town with other people, see building with friends.

Your data stays put

Steps, pushes and workout data are read from Apple Health on your phone, only to work out your points. None of it is sent to us or anyone else. You can read exactly what StepHaven reads in the privacy policy.

What you need

  • An iPhone running iOS 26.5 or later to play StepHaven and read Apple Health. Carrying your iPhone is enough for ordinary walking steps.
  • An Apple Watch in wheelchair mode for recorded wheelchair pushes, plus permission for StepHaven to read them.
  • For Ember, light it before the workout and allow the requested Health access. Apple Watch is recommended; earning depends on qualifying exercise minutes and active calories reaching Health during the Ember.
